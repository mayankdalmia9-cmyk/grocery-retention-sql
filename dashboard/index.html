What turns a first grocery order into a weekly habit

Six findings from 1.47M item-level transactions of a US grocery retailer, written as a growth team would act on them: onboarding, retention, win-back and campaign measurement.

01 · Onboarding

3+ trips in the first 28 days makes a household 2.3× as likely to be a year-end regular

Regular = shopped in at least 4 of weeks 45–52. Households first seen by week 40 (n = 2,447). 74% of 3+ trip households were regulars vs 33% of those with 1–2.

ActDesign onboarding around the 2nd and 3rd order inside four weeks, not just the first.

02 · Cohort retention

Newer households keep 63% in month 1, against 89% for the established base

% of each first-month cohort that shopped N months later. The January cohort is mostly long-standing customers because the data starts on 1 Jan; Feb–Nov cohorts are the closest proxy for new customers.

ActReport new-customer cohorts separately. A blended retention rate hides where the leak is.

03 · Fresh categories

Early produce buyers are 6–12 points likelier to stay, at the same trip frequency

Bought produce in first 28 daysNo produce

Compared within the same early-frequency band so the gap isn't just "frequent shoppers buy everything". Still a correlation.

ActA/B test a produce-led first-order offer and measure 8-week regular rate.

04 · Win-back

The odds of a lapsed household returning halve between week 1 and week 8

Of households already away k weeks, the share that shopped again within the next 4 weeks. Gaps starting by week 36.

ActFire win-back messages at 2–4 weeks of inactivity, while the return rate is still above 60%.

05 · Wallet share

Households shopping 12+ departments spend 6× more a year and 50% more per trip

Departments = PRODUCE, MEAT, DELI, PASTRY and so on. Frequent shoppers naturally cover more departments, so treat this as a lever to test.

ActCross-category nudges (e.g. bakery with dairy) to grow basket width, measured against a holdout.

06 · Campaign measurement

Naive measurement says the campaigns did nothing. Matched by prior spend, they lifted spend +19 to +26 points

Campaign 8 (May–Jun)Campaign 13 (Aug–Sep)Campaign 18 (Oct–Dec)

Lift = targeted households' % spend change minus untargeted households' change, within the same pre-campaign spend quintile. Targeted households spent ~3× more before each campaign, so a raw comparison is biased. Weighted lift: $29–59 per targeted household.

ActAlways keep a random holdout. Lift was near zero for top spenders, so spend campaign budget on low and mid spenders.
